## Explore Zhuifen Station

Zhuifen Station is known for its historic timber station building, railway platforms and the auspicious pairing of the station names “Zhuifen” and “Chenggong” (“pursue success”). In contrast to Wanggaoliao's broad city views, this is a place to slow down and notice a working railway's old architecture. Visit by day before heading to Wanggaoliao for sunset.

### Visiting tips

- **Allow 30–45 minutes** for a look around, excluding train waiting time.
- **Visit in daylight** to see eaves, woodwork and the platform setting.
- **Check tickets:** Commemorative ticket products, types and availability can change. Confirm with [Taiwan Railways](https://www.railway.gov.tw/).

### Highlights

1. **Timber station:** Notice its roof, eaves and human-scale forecourt.
2. **“Pursue success”:** The combination of two station names has become a railway-travel souvenir theme.
3. **Working station:** Respect passengers, railway staff and train movements while taking photographs.

## About Zhuifen Station and its management

Zhuifen is an active stop on Taichung's coastal railway line in Dadu District. Its historic wooden architecture and everyday transport function are both part of the experience. **The state-owned Taiwan Railway Corporation manages station premises, ticketing, platforms and operational safety.** Visitors may admire the old eaves and frontage but may not enter restricted railway spaces. Consult the [official station record](https://tip.railway.gov.tw/tra-tip-web/tip/tip00H/tipH41/viewStaInfo/2260).

## Opening hours and tickets

Station services, ticket sales, train stops and platform access do not necessarily share identical schedules. For the “Zhuifen–Chenggong” ticket tradition, check the [station page and timetable](https://tip.railway.gov.tw/tra-tip-web/tip/tip00H/tipH41/viewStaInfo/2260). Exterior viewing from lawful public areas may be free; train trips and ticket-controlled zones follow current Taiwan Railways fares. Commemorative tickets are not complimentary souvenirs, and supply can change.

## Getting there

**By train:** Arriving on Taiwan Railways is the most fitting way to experience the site. Check routes, stops, fares and your return train on the [official website](https://www.railway.gov.tw/), not just a map's estimated frequency.

**By car:** Navigate to the Zhuifen Street address, park according to local signs and keep station entrances and residential access clear. Travel to Wanggaoliao requires a separate road journey.

## Suggested station walk

1. **Station forecourt:** View the whole building and its streetscape from a safe pedestrian area.
2. **Timber details:** Photograph eaves, windows, doors and name signs without entering restricted rooms.
3. **Platforms and trains:** Access only where tickets and station rules permit; never cross the yellow line or tracks.
4. **Nearby heritage:** Combine with [Jukuiju](/en/attractions/jukuiju/) before continuing to [Wanggaoliao](/en/) in the evening.

## Railway history and architectural photography

Zhuifen's history is tied to the development of Taichung's coastal railway. The proportions and timber details of the old station evoke earlier local transport. The playful pairing with Chenggong Station later became a symbol of good wishes. Photograph the whole wooden facade, structural elements and station signs without blocking commuters or entering the track or operations area.

## Safety and accommodation

This remains an active station, so obey railway warnings. Check last trains and transfers if making a rail-based trip, especially before an evening in Wanggaoliao. Accommodation in Wuri, near Taichung HSR station or downtown can suit the following day; a brief station stop need not dictate a hotel change.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is Zhuifen the same station as Chenggong?

No. They are distinct stations; “Zhuifen–Chenggong” is the auspicious combination of their names.

### May I step onto the tracks for photos?

No. The railway is operational. Stay strictly within permitted public or ticketed areas.

### Can I combine this with Wanggaoliao?

Yes. Visit the station and cultural sights during the day, then travel separately to Wanggaoliao for evening views.
