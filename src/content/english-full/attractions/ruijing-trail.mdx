## Exploring Dadu Ruijing Hiking Trail

The Ruijing Hiking Trail runs through plateau hills beside Ruijing Village, making it possible to combine community history and a woodland hike on the same day. Unlike Wanggaoliao's fixed viewing platform, this trail is better suited to observing shade, earthen slopes and hill vegetation. The city government has previously described a connection between Ruijing Trail and Longjing's Zhukeng–Nanliao Trail; actual routes should still be determined by entrance maps and access conditions on the day.

### Visitor advice and trail highlights

- **Suggested stay:** 60–120 minutes, depending on the section, return journey and personal fitness.
- **Equipment:** Non-slip walking shoes, water, sun protection and an offline map. Watch out for slippery ground after rain.
- **What to observe:** Woodland plants, gradients, the plateau's red-earth terrain and the relative heights of nearby settlements.

## About Ruijing Hiking Trail

The path connects the natural landscape of the Dadu Plateau with the culture of its settlements. Its character comes from walking in the hills beside a living community—not from a large temple or a ticketed tourist complex.

### Who manages the trail?

**Management:** Taichung City Government Tourism and Travel Bureau and the relevant trail authorities. Municipal tourism information has previously reported repairs to Ruijing Trail and links with nearby paths. Entrance access, maintenance, closures and safety rules follow current announcements and signs. [Taichung Tourism](https://travel.taichung.gov.tw/zh-tw/attractions/intro/1151).

## Opening hours, admission and transport

Outdoor trails do not usually have a ticket booth, but weather, construction or damage to the path may restrict access. Drivers should search the complete name “大肚瑞井登山步道” and choose lawful parking near the actual trailhead. When connecting from a bus, check the last departure and walking distance first. **Do not mistake the nearby Great Wall Trail entrance for the Ruijing entrance.**

## Discover its appeal: terrain, ecology and local history

### The Dadu Plateau landscape

The route crosses plateau slopes and green shade, making it possible to compare views from different heights. Soil, exposed roots and steps can make progress substantially slower than on flat ground.

### Connection with Ruijing Village

The [old wells and homes of Ruijing](/en/attractions/ruijing-village/) beside the hills record how people obtained water and lived in the past. Combining the two stops helps explain how natural terrain shaped the settlement.

## Suggested walking route

1. **Confirm the trailhead:** Check the local route diagram, construction information and weather.
2. **Woodland walk:** Follow clear waymarks and observe vegetation on the slopes; do not take shortcuts.
3. **Turn back in good time:** Reserve energy for the return along the same path rather than forcing a crossing onto an unfamiliar branch.
4. **Community walk:** Continue to [Dadu Ruijing Village](/en/attractions/ruijing-village/) to learn about its wells and architecture.

## Visitor etiquette, photography and accommodation

Wide rest areas are suitable for photographing tree shadows and layers of sloping terrain. Never trample the hillside or climb over railings. Afternoon thunderstorms and darkness increase the risk; complete the route in daylight where possible. Choose accommodation in Dadu, Longjing or central Taichung based on transport convenience for the next destination.

### Are Ruijing Historic Well and Ruijing Trail the same place?

No. The old well is a cultural site in the settlement, while the trail is a hillside path. They can be visited together but need **separate navigation destinations**.

### Should I hike after rain?

Avoid the path when there is a risk of slippery ground, rockfall or heavy rain. Adjust your visit according to local management notices and weather warnings.
