## Exploring Ruijing Village

Ruijing Village lies on the Dadu Plateau and is known for its old wells, traditional red-brick homes and memories of everyday hillside community life. Instead of Wanggaoliao's far-reaching panorama, this is a place to walk slowly through lanes and observe how residents lived on elevated ground where obtaining water was difficult. The wells are important evidence of the plateau's water history, but **not every well opening is a public tourist facility**.

### Visitor advice and highlights

- **Suggested stay:** 40–80 minutes to see the outside of village buildings on foot.
- **What to observe:** Old well structures, brick walls, traditional doors and windows, and the differences in elevation between village lanes.
- **Photography etiquette:** Do not enter homes or photograph residents' private lives; do not use lane entrances as long-term parking spaces.

## About Ruijing Village

The wells and traditional homes reflect the living conditions of the Dadu Plateau. Ruijing is still an everyday residential community, **not a fully open-air folk museum**. Appreciate its cultural landscape from public roads and legally accessible facilities.

### Who manages the area?

**Management:** Dadu District Office, Ruijing's local community administration and the authorities responsible for individual facilities. Public services, roads and individual historic wells may be managed separately. Respect residents' privacy and posted instructions. [Taichung Tourism source](https://travel.taichung.gov.tw/zh-tw/attractions/intro/964).

## Opening hours, admission and transport

There is no single ticket counter for this village. Public lanes may be used in accordance with normal traffic rules. If a well or historic structure is behind a fence or gate, follow the posted restrictions. Navigate using “瑞井古井” (Ruijing Historic Well), rather than searching only for Wanggaoliao. Hill roads can be narrow: park at a lawful location and continue on foot. Check actual bus departures before relying on public transport.

### Three separate Google Maps locations

- **[Well No. 1 (一號古井)](https://maps.app.goo.gl/3BnLSNeDZdfphAMQ6):** Use this specific pin for one of the historic wells. Observe the rim and surrounding structure from a public, safe position; never cross a barrier.
- **[Ruijing Historic Well interpretation sign (瑞井古井說明牌)](https://maps.app.goo.gl/CNdae42tFPBZTU5eA):** Read the on-site explanation of local history and water use, then compare it with the well features. The sign and well have distinct map pins.
- **[Ruijing century-old frangipani (瑞井 百年緬梔)](https://maps.app.goo.gl/JJtwiLJUHvwPjs6S9):** Observe this old tree as part of the village streetscape. Do not climb, break branches or disturb nearby homes.

The three pins help orient a walk through the settlement; they do not establish vehicle access or parking. Verify public access and the walking connections on site.

## Discover its appeal: water, architecture and community

### Historic wells and water sources

The plateau terrain made access to water a major challenge in the past. Old wells are more than photo subjects: they bear witness to the relationship between local people and water resources. Never climb onto a well rim, throw anything into a well or draw water without permission.

### Red-brick houses and village lanes

Roof tiles, brickwork, door lintels and the turns of narrow lanes reveal long-standing traces of settlement life. Unlike a large commercial tourist district, Ruijing's appeal lies in its quiet, small-scale character and the fact that residents continue to use these spaces.

## Suggested walking route

1. **Public arrival point and [historic well sign](https://maps.app.goo.gl/CNdae42tFPBZTU5eA):** Identify lawful parking and read the water-history interpretation.
2. **[Well No. 1](https://maps.app.goo.gl/3BnLSNeDZdfphAMQ6):** Observe the well and nearby village setting from a safe, lawful position.
3. **[Century-old frangipani](https://maps.app.goo.gl/JJtwiLJUHvwPjs6S9) and old-house lanes:** Follow public streets to see the tree and architecture without crossing private courtyards.
4. **Hiking extension:** If fitness and trail conditions allow, connect to the [Dadu Ruijing Hiking Trail](/en/attractions/ruijing-trail/).

## Visitor etiquette, photography and accommodation

Using a medium or telephoto lens for details visible from public lanes is better than peering into private courtyards. Morning natural light suits brick textures. Any local guided walk is subject to the organiser's latest notice. Choose accommodation in Longjing, Dadu or Wuri based on the following day's transport arrangements.

### Is there only one well in Ruijing?

The settlement name is closely connected to its well culture, but signs referring to different sites should not be mistaken for one entrance. Follow local interpretation boards and individual map locations.

### Can I visit Ruijing and Wanggaoliao on the same day?

Yes. A daytime cultural walk works well before transferring to Wanggaoliao in the evening. Allow travel time by road between the two.
