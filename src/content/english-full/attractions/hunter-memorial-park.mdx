## Exploring Dr Hunter Memorial Park

Dr Hunter Memorial Park on the Dadu Plateau in Longjing District commemorates **American professor James Hunter**, who helped residents improve their access to water. This is not simply a green place with a view. The story worth pausing to understand is the Dadu hillside's former water shortage, people carrying water by hand and the community effort to build a water-supply system.

### Visitor tips and highlights

- **Suggested stay:** 30–60 minutes, perhaps paired with a neighbouring village or trail.
- **What to observe:** The commemorative statue of Dr Hunter, public recreation facilities and surrounding plateau terrain.
- **Cultural connection:** Combine the park with [Ruijing's historic wells](/en/attractions/ruijing-village/) to understand water access during different periods on the same plateau.

## About Dr Hunter Memorial Park

According to the municipal tourism authority, Professor Hunter helped Dadu Plateau residents raise funds for a running-water project and donated his own savings. The water-supply works were completed in **1963 (ROC year 52)**. Residents later established the park and commemorative statue in gratitude for his contribution to better living conditions.

### Who manages the park?

**Management:** The local public-facilities authorities responsible for the Longjing District park. Maintenance, cleaning, activities and repairs are handled by the authority responsible on site; the historical background follows the Taichung City Government Tourism and Travel Bureau's account. [Official reference](https://travel.taichung.gov.tw/zh-tw/attractions/intro/1388).

## Opening hours, admission and transport

Official tourism data describes the outdoor park as open around the clock, but individual facilities may be restricted during maintenance. There is no general entrance fee. Drivers should navigate to the **park's own name and address**, not reuse the Wanggaoliao map pin. Before using public transport, verify services and the final walking section.

## Discover its appeal: history, memorial design and community

### Water shortages and public infrastructure

Before dependable piped water, obtaining household water was extremely difficult for people living atop the Dadu hills. Professor Hunter and residents' efforts reflect how plateau settlements improved public health and everyday living conditions.

### Memorial statue and public space

The statue provides a starting point for understanding this history. Read any on-site interpretation before looking at the surrounding terrain. These public facilities are not an indoor memorial museum; respect residents using the park for exercise and rest.

## Suggested route

1. **Park entrance:** Locate accessible paths and rest areas.
2. **Memorial statue:** Learn about Professor Hunter and the historical water-supply challenges on the Dadu hills.
3. **Surrounding terrain:** Observe the plateau slopes and think about the transport difficulties involved in fetching water in earlier times.
4. **Continue exploring:** Visit [Ruijing's historic wells](/en/attractions/ruijing-village/) or the [Zhukeng–Nanliao Trail](/en/attractions/zhukeng-nanliao-trail/).

## Visitor etiquette, photography and accommodation

Choose angles that do not obstruct other park users when photographing the memorial. Do not climb on the statue or other facilities. A daytime visit is easiest; after dark, adjust according to lighting and safety conditions. Stay in Longjing or central Taichung depending on the next day's route.

### Why does the park commemorate Dr Hunter?

He helped the people of the Dadu Plateau improve their supply of running water. The memorial and park preserve local memory of that contribution.

### Is it the same place as the Wanggaoliao night view?

No. Hunter Memorial Park is in Longjing District; Wanggaoliao is a plateau viewpoint in Nantun District. Navigate to each separately.
