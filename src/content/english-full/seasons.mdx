## Spring (March–May)

- **Weather and visibility:** Frontal systems and moist air bring variable cloud and fog, especially near dawn.
- **Sunset:** Roughly **18:00–18:30**; arrive about an hour early.
- **Nature:** Plants in the Chun She area and silvergrass-covered slopes change with spring growth. Wet trails can be slippery.
- **Best for:** Blue-hour scenes or a clear night following rain. Prolonged rain is not ideal for groups with older visitors.
- **Beware:** Dense early-morning fog, sometimes around **04:00–08:00**, can severely reduce road visibility. Postpone ridge driving in poor conditions.

## Summer (June–August)

- **Weather:** Afternoon thunderstorms are common; clearer conditions sometimes follow as convection fades in the evening.
- **Sunset:** Approximately **18:40–19:00**, the latest period of the year.
- **Nature:** Mosquitoes and other insects are active. Wear long trousers and repellant near tall grass; avoid bare feet.
- **Best for:** Short blue-hour sessions and clear views when storms have fully passed.
- **Beware:** Typhoon warnings may close the park. Never stay on an exposed ridge during lightning or intense rain; follow Taichung City notices.

## Autumn (September–November)

- **Weather:** Conditions can settle into drier, clearer air after the early northeast-monsoon transition; often one of the strongest seasons for distant views.
- **Sunset:** Approximately **17:10–18:00**, earlier as the season advances.
- **Nature:** Silvergrass turns golden and migrating birds may pass overhead. Use binoculars and avoid shouting around parking or habitats.
- **Best for:** Sunset, blue hour and night photography, particularly in late autumn.
- **Beware:** Remnants of tropical systems still matter; check the Central Weather Administration forecast for the actual week.

## Winter (December–February)

- **Weather:** Cold-air outbreaks can bring low cloud; clear mornings after a front sometimes offer fog or cloud-sea scenery, but never reliably.
- **Sunset:** Approximately **17:10–17:30**; darkness arrives early.
- **Nature and comfort:** Strong ridge winds can make an elevation below 300 m feel much colder; bring warm, windproof layers.
- **Best for:** Crisp, clear winter nights with contrasting warm city lights and cool sky tones.
- **Beware:** Older people, children and people sensitive to cold should plan shorter stays. Strong gusts make exposed edges unsafe.

## Questions and tips

- **When might a sea of clouds appear?** Sometimes on clear mornings after a winter cold front; it is not a standard daily feature, and dawn driving requires caution.
- **Photography preference:** October, November and September often offer promising combinations, but individual clear days in other months can be excellent.
- **Poor visibility does not always mean no city lights:** Clouds can diffuse the glow for a softer, hazier image.
- **Clear sky is not enough:** Poor AQI can wash out distant buildings even when there are few clouds.

## Common planning mistakes

1. Checking sunshine but ignoring air quality.
2. Reading only a twelve-hour forecast rather than hourly changes.
3. Assuming all spring afternoons are suitable despite thunderstorm risk.
4. Dismissing winter, when a clear post-front night may be beautiful.
