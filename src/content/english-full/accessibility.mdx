---
title: "Accessibility and Senior Visitor Guide"
---

**An accessible viewing area has been installed, and the main paths have been made smoother. However, the grassy slopes, stairs and some side paths are not accessible from end to end. If travelling with older people, a wheelchair or a stroller, use the main route and return the same way.**

## Suggested four-step route with fewer changes in elevation

1. **Entrance and parking:** Arrive early and choose an officially permitted parking space close to the main entrance.
2. **Smooth main route:** Enter through the main gate and follow the paved or timber-boardwalk path to the accessible viewing area; do not cut across the grassy slope.
3. **Viewing and resting:** Stop around the level viewing area and benches. There is no need to tackle stairs just to reach a higher viewpoint.
4. **Return by the same route:** Identify the return path before it gets dark and follow the same smooth path back to the parking area.

**Avoid:** Grassy slopes, stair shortcuts, slippery surfaces after rain and unlit edge paths. Wheelchair users are advised to travel with a companion.

## Terrain overview

The Taichung City Government's [2020 improvement project description](https://www.construction.taichung.gov.tw/27769/27862/27865/27868/27877/1581813) covers an accessible viewing area, smoother park paths, renewed seating, stair handrails and low-level lighting. The work made the main route more visitor-friendly, but it does not mean every slope, staircase or viewpoint is wheelchair-accessible.

## Wheelchairs and visitors with reduced mobility

- Arrive early and prioritise a legally available parking place near the entrance and main walkway.
- Once inside, choose the wide, level, continuous main route. Do not enter grassy slopes or stairs simply to reach the highest point.
- Ask a companion for assistance if there is a noticeable gradient, slippery surface or crowd. Electric wheelchair users should reserve sufficient battery charge for the return.
- On a first visit, enter while it is still light. Inspect the return surface and slopes before deciding whether to stay after dark.

## Strollers and young children

- The main paths and level viewing area are more suitable for strollers. Avoid stair shortcuts and the grassy slope.
- Timber decking, grass and inclines may be slippery after rain. Use the stroller's brake strap and never leave it parked on a slope.
- Visitor numbers concentrate around sunset on weekends; arriving earlier or visiting on a weekday is more relaxed.

## Visiting with older people

- You do not have to reach the very top of the grassy slope. A comfortable stop can be arranged around the accessible viewing area and main walkway.
- Use the park benches for breaks and bring a lightweight seat cushion if useful, but do not place temporary chairs in the walking route.
- The wind feels stronger than in the city. Bring a jacket, drinking water and regularly used medicines. Start the return before anyone becomes exhausted.

## Restrooms and limitations after dark

The park has public toilets, but this guide has not found sufficiently recent official specifications to verify **accessible toilet dimensions, grab rails or opening hours**. If these facilities are essential, consider using a suitable toilet in the city before travelling and identifying an alternative in advance.

Low-level lighting helps visitors follow the main paths but does not make the entire park as bright as an indoor space. After dark, carry a torch, travel with a companion and return along the same known route rather than taking a dark shortcut for the first time.

If a member of your group has particular needs concerning gradients, toilets or night vision, the safest plan is a short daytime visit, extending it only if conditions on site are comfortable. See the [restrooms, paths and facilities overview](/en/facilities/).
